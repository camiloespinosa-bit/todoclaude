Cheat sheet

Zero · always true

If + present, present

Facts, rules, policies, how a system works. If = when.

If a client pays late, we charge a 2% fee.

If demand goes up, prices rise.

First · real possibility

If + present, will / can / may + verb

Real plans, offers, warnings, promises about a specific future. Unless = if ... not.

If you sign today, we'll give you free delivery.

Unless we hire two people, we won't hit the target.

Second · imagined

If + past, would / could + verb

Hypothetical or unlikely situations, advice, dreams. The past form does not mean past time.

If I were the CEO, I would cut useless meetings.

If we had more budget, we could open in Miami.

Spanish speaker traps

If you will send it, I'll check it.If you send it, I'll check it. (no will after if)
If I would have time, I would help.If I had time, I would help. (no would after if)
If I am the manager, I would...If I were the manager, I would... (second = past form)
Unless you don't pay, we'll cancel.Unless you pay, we'll cancel. (unless already means "if not")
What would you do if you lose your job?What would you do if you lost your job?

Comma: use one when the if-clause goes first (If you sign today, we'll...). No comma when it goes second (We'll ... if you sign today). "If I was" is common in speech; "If I were" is the safe choice in formal business English.
